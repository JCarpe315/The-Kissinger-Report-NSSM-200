# The Kissinger Report: A Complete Forensic Guide to NSSM 200

## What This Document Is

This is a comprehensive, evidence-based investigation into **NSSM 200** — National Security Study Memorandum 200, also known as "The Kissinger Report" — and everything that surrounded it, followed it, and grew from it. Nothing here is speculation. Every claim is tied to a source. If something is uncertain, it is labeled as such.

NSSM 200 was not a speech, not a press release, and not a suggestion. It was a **classified U.S. national security study** that treated population growth in poor countries as a threat to American economic and strategic interests, then built an institutional machinery to reduce that growth. The machinery still operates today, though its name, its packaging, and its public face have changed.

This guide is written for anyone who wants to understand what actually happened — the documents, the people, the money, the countries targeted, the methods used, the health effects, and the modern programs that continue the same work under different branding.

## Part 1: The Three Documents

### 1.1 NSSM 200 — The Order (April 24, 1974)

**What it was:** A memorandum from Henry Kissinger, then Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs, ordering a classified interagency study.

**Who received it:** The Secretary of Defense, the Secretary of Agriculture, the Director of Central Intelligence (the head of the CIA), the Deputy Secretary of State, and the Administrator of the Agency for International Development (USAID).

**What it asked for:** A study looking forward to at least the year 2000, using several alternative projections of population growth. For each projection, the study was to assess:
- The corresponding pace of development, especially in poorer countries.
- The demand for U.S. exports, especially food, and trade problems arising from competition for resources.
- The likelihood that population growth or imbalances would produce disruptive foreign policies and international instability.

**A critical instruction:** The study was explicitly told to focus on "the international political and economic implications of population growth rather than its ecological, sociological or other aspects." This was not a humanitarian study. It was a resource-security study framed in the language of national security.

**What it also asked for:** Possible courses of action for the United States in dealing with population matters abroad, particularly in developing countries, including:
- What new initiatives were needed to focus international attention on the "population problem."
- Whether technological innovations or development could reduce growth or ameliorate its effects.
- Whether the United States could improve its assistance in the population field, and if so, in what form and through which agencies — bilateral, multilateral, or private.

**The deadline:** The study was to be forwarded to the President no later than May 29, 1974.

**Source:** FRUS 1969–1976, Vol. E–14, Pt. 1, Doc. 113. (See Part 13 for full archival citation.)

### 1.2 NSSM 200 — The Study (December 10, 1974)

**What it was:** A 198-page classified interagency paper titled *"Implications of Worldwide Population Growth For U.S. Security and Overseas Interests."* This is the document commonly called "The Kissinger Report."

**Who wrote it:** Multiple agencies contributed. The study was directed by the NSC Under Secretaries Committee. The primary drafter has been identified in some sources as Philander Claxton, a State Department population specialist who had written to Assistant Secretary Joseph Sisco in 1968, though this attribution requires further archival verification. The document was classified by Harry C. Blaney III.

**Classification:** Classified CONFIDENTIAL. Declassified on July 3, 1989, by F. Graboske of the National Security Council, under Executive Order 12356 (EO 12356). The document had originally been scheduled for automatic declassification on December 31, 1989, under Executive Order 11652 (EO 11652).

**A note on declassification:** The report was briefly declassified in 1976 through the efforts of Werner Fornos, President of the Population Institute, with the aid of several members of Congress. However, Fornos was unable to achieve any press coverage. The report was then **reclassified** as a result of objections from "members of the national security establishment." It remained classified for **14 years** rather than the recommended 5 years. The 1989 declassification apparently resulted from a Freedom of Information Act request. The report was never printed; only a handful of photocopies existed.

**Full text location:** USAID PDF [PCAAB500.pdf](https://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PCAAB500.pdf), also archived at the National Archives NSC Institutional Files, Box H-204.

**Structure of the study:**
- **Executive Summary** (pages 4–17)
- **Part One: Analytical Section**
  - Chapter I: World Demographic Trends
  - Chapter II: Population and World Food Supplies
  - Chapter III: Minerals and Fuel
  - Chapter IV: Economic Development and Population Growth
  - Chapter V: Implications for National Security
  - Chapter VI: World Population Conference
- **Part Two: Policy Recommendations**
  - Section I: U.S. Global Population Strategy
  - Section II: Action to Create Conditions for Fertility Decline
  - Section III: International Organizations and Multilateral Programs
  - Section IV: Provision and Development of Family Planning Services
  - Section V: Research to Improve Fertility Control Technology
  - Section VI: Action to Develop Worldwide Political Commitment

**Key quantitative claims in the Executive Summary:**
- World population growth rate: approximately 2% per year, compared to about 1% before World War II, under 0.5% in 1750–1900, and far lower rates before 1750.
- The effect was to double the world's population in 35 years instead of 100 years.
- Almost 80 million people were being added each year, compared with 10 million in 1900.
- Since 1950, population in developed countries had been growing at 0 to 1.5% per year, while in less developed countries (LDCs) it was growing at 2.0 to 3.5% (doubling in 20 to 35 years).
- Because of the momentum of population dynamics, reductions in birth rates affect total numbers only slowly. High birth rates in the recent past had resulted in a high proportion in the youngest age groups, so substantial population increases would continue even if a two-child family became the norm.
- The study's aim: for the world to achieve **replacement level of fertility (replacement-level fertility)** (a two-child family on average) by about the year 2000.
- This would require the present 2% growth rate to decline to 1.7% within a decade and to 1.1% by 2000.
- Compared to the UN medium projection, this goal would result in **500 million fewer people in 2000** and about **3 billion fewer in 2050**.

**The study's listed causes of high birth rates:**
- Inadequate information about and availability of means of fertility control.
- Inadequate motivation for reduced numbers of children combined with motivation for many children resulting from still-high infant and child mortality and need for support in old age.
- The status of women.
- The "value" of children in providing labor and old-age security.
- The absence of educational and employment opportunities for women.

**Recommended conditions for fertility decline included:**
- Education, especially for women.
- Lower infant and child mortality.
- Wage work for women.
- Alternatives to children as old-age support.
- "Education and indoctrination of the rising generation of children regarding the desirability of smaller family size."

**The "Alternative View" — Paragraph 37:**

This is one of the most important passages in the entire document. The Executive Summary includes a paragraph that presents — without rejection — a far more radical position, mentioning "mandatory programs" and "tight control of our food resources" as policy options. (For the full quote and analysis, see Part 7.1.)

**What the study does NOT say:**
- It does not mention compulsory vaccination.
- It does not mention genetic modification of children.
- It does not mention "extermination services."
- These are later conspiracy accretions not found in the actual document.

**The mineral resource link:**

Chapter III of the study explicitly connects population policy to U.S. access to raw materials:

> "The US economy will require large and increasing amounts of **mineral resources** from abroad, especially from less developed countries. That fact gives the US enhanced interest in the political, economic and social stability of the supplying countries. Wherever a lessening of population pressures through reduced birth rates can increase the prospects for such stability, population policy becomes relevant to resource supplies and to economic interests of the United States."

The document listed U.S. import dependence on strategic commodities from developing countries:
- **Copper:** Canada 31%, Peru 27%, Chile 22%
- **Iron:** Canada 50%, Venezuela 31%
- **Tin:** Malaysia 64%, Thailand 27%
- **Zinc (Ore):** Canada 60%, Mexico 24%
- **Petroleum (crude):** Canada 42%, Venezuela 17%
- **Lead:** Canada 29%, Peru 21%, Australia 21%
- **Nickel:** Canada 82%, Norway 8%
- **Bauxite:** Africa, South America
- **Aluminum:** Canada 76%

The study warned that leaders of less developed countries might "see developed country pressures for family planning as a form of economic or racial imperialism; this would create quite a serious backlash."

### 1.3 NSDM 314 — The Decision (November 26, 1975)

**What it was:** National Security Decision Memorandum 314, issued over the signature of Brent Scowcroft (then Deputy Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs), adopting the NSSM 200 study as official U.S. policy.

**Who received it:** The Secretary of State, the Secretary of the Treasury, the Secretary of Defense, the Secretary of Agriculture, the Secretary of Health, Education and Welfare, and the Administrator of AID.

**What it did:**
- **Endorsed** the NSSM 200 Executive Summary recommendations as U.S. policy.
- **Rejected** paragraph 31(c) — a recommendation for announcing a U.S. national population goal — as outside the scope of NSSM 200.
- **Modified** paragraph 31(b) — concerning world population goals — to state that "the general goal of achieving global replacement levels of fertility by the year 2000 does not imply interference in the national policies of other countries."
- **Assigned** the Chairman of the NSC Under Secretaries Committee the responsibility to define, develop, and coordinate population policy.
- **Mandated** an initial report within six months, then **annual reports** thereafter.
- **Added** ex officio members to the Under Secretaries Committee for population purposes: the Council on Environmental Quality, the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), and the President's Science Adviser.
- **Directed** a funding review for FY 1976 onwards.

**Source:** FRUS 1969–1976, Vol. E–14, Pt. 1, Doc. 122. (See Part 13 for full archival citation.)

## Part 2: Origins — Where Did This Come From?

NSSM 200 did not emerge from a vacuum. It was the culmination of more than two decades of organized elite advocacy, foundation funding, and incremental government policy shifts.

### 2.1 The Population Council (1952)

**John D. Rockefeller III** founded the Population Council in 1952 after the Rockefeller Foundation trustees rejected his proposal to fund birth control research for developing countries. The Council's first administrator was **Frederick Osborn**, founder of the American Eugenics Society and trustee of the Pioneer Fund — an organization with deep eugenics roots.

By 1958, a State Department digest warned that explosive population growth could produce political conflict in less developed countries — language that would be recycled almost verbatim in NSSM 200 sixteen years later.

### 2.2 The Draper Committee (1958)

President Eisenhower formed the Committee to Study the U.S. Military Assistance Program, chaired by retired Major General **William H. Draper Jr.** (former director of the European Recovery Program). Draper concluded that long-term economic aid to poor nations could succeed **only if accompanied by population control programs**. Eisenhower and the U.S. Council of Catholic Bishops disavowed the report, but the intellectual framework was established.

### 2.3 The Rockefeller Commission (1969–1972)

In July 1969, President Nixon presented a "Special Message to the Congress on Problems of Population Growth." In March 1970, he signed legislation creating the **Commission on Population Growth and the American Future**, chaired by **John D. Rockefeller III**.

The Commission's March 27, 1972 report, *"Population and the American Future,"* was 360 pages with six volumes of supporting materials. It called for:
- Population education in schools.
- A National Institute of Population Sciences within NIH.
- An Office of Population Growth and Distribution within the Executive Office of the President.
- A Council of Social Advisers to monitor demographic variables.
- **Legalization of abortion** at home and abroad.

Nixon **rejected** the Commission's abortion recommendations, but the report normalized the framing of population growth as a national problem requiring federal intervention.

### 2.4 Congressional Action (1966–1970)

**1966:** Congress passed the Foreign Assistance Act, earmarking USAID funds for population control programs abroad. The legislation directed that **all U.S. economic aid be made contingent** upon foreign governments' willingness to cooperate with State Department desires for population initiatives within their borders.

**1969:** President Nixon established a separate **Office of Population within USAID** with a $50 million annual budget.

**1970:** Title X of the Public Health Service Act passed, providing family planning services for low-income women and men domestically. (For full legislative history and sponsor details, see Part 8.2.)

### 2.5 The Bucharest Conference (August 1974)

The **World Population Conference** adopted the **World Population Plan of Action** by consensus of 137 nations. NSSM 200 explicitly piggybacked on this framework, using it as diplomatic cover. The study states: "The World Population Plan of Action and the resolutions adopted by consensus by 137 nations at the August 1974 U.N. World Population Conference, though not ideal, provide an excellent framework for developing a worldwide system of population/family planning programs."

**Critical timing:** NSSM 200 was ordered in April 1974, before Bucharest. The study was completed in December 1974, after Bucharest. This means the U.S. was already drafting its unilateral national security strategy while participating in the "consensus" multilateral process.

## Part 3: The Architects and Operators

### 3.1 The Drafters

**Philander Claxton** — Identified by some sources as the primary drafter of NSSM 200. A 1968 document shows a "population specialist Philander Claxton" writing to Assistant Secretary of State Joseph Sisco. However, the name "Philander Claxton" also refers to a U.S. Commissioner of Education who died in 1957, creating confusion. The exact role of the 1974 drafter requires further archival verification.

**Harry C. Blaney III** — The classifier named on the document's cover page. Died May 11, 2023, in Bethesda, Maryland, age 85. B.A. Allegheny College (1959), M.A. Yale (1961). Joined the Foreign Service in December 1965. Served as special assistant to Daniel Patrick Moynihan (then counselor to the president) and to Russell E. Train (then chairman of the Council on Environmental Quality). Member of the policy planning staff of Secretaries of State Henry Kissinger and Cyrus Vance, covering energy, nonproliferation, technology, and environmental issues. In retirement, president/CEO of the Coalition of American Leadership Abroad and senior fellow at the Center for International Policy.

### 3.2 The Approvers

**Robert S. Ingersoll** (also referred to as Robert Ingersoll) — Chairman, NSC Under Secretaries Committee. Submitted the completed study to President Ford on December 14, 1974. Ingersoll was not a career diplomat. He was CEO and Chairman of **Borg-Warner Corporation** (a major automotive and industrial manufacturer) before Nixon appointed him Ambassador to Japan (1972–1973), then Assistant Secretary of State for East Asian and Pacific Affairs (1974), then Deputy Secretary of State (July 1974 – March 1976). His business background is significant: NSSM 200's framing of population growth as a threat to U.S. commercial interests and mineral access aligns with the perspective of a manufacturing CEO.

**Charles W. Robinson** — Succeeded Ingersoll as Deputy Secretary of State and USC Chairman. Submitted the **First Annual Report** on July 29, 1976. On September 23, 1976, Robinson circulated NSC memorandum **NSC–U/N–178** to Committee Members, explicitly proposing that U.S. food assistance be used as leverage to pressure recipient countries on population control.

### 3.3 The Implementers

**Marshall Green** — Named **Coordinator of Population Affairs** at the State Department on **December 3, 1975**. Green was a career Foreign Service Officer who had served as Assistant Secretary of State for East Asian and Pacific Affairs (1969–1973) — the position Ingersoll briefly held before becoming Deputy Secretary. Green had previously been Ambassador to Australia (1973–1975). After retiring in 1979, he joined the board of the **Population Crisis Committee** (now Population Action International).

On **December 15, 1975**, U.S. ambassadors worldwide were instructed to assist in implementing NSDM 314 and to assess population growth concerns in their host nations.

On **April 13, 1976**, Green submitted a memorandum to Kissinger (FRUS Document 124) listing specific actions where Kissinger could be helpful in curbing world population growth. This included:
- A draft cable to all diplomatic posts setting forth Kissinger's concern about world population growth (approved by Kissinger on May 7, 1976; sent as **telegram 113982** to all posts on May 10, 1976).
- Suggested language for Kissinger's UNCTAD speech relating to primary health services, status of women, and food-for-work projects.
- Talking points for Kissinger's forthcoming African trip.
- A recommendation that Kissinger address a meeting of approximately **150 non-governmental population groups**, either under State Department auspices or organized by the **World Population Society**.

### 3.4 The Operators in the Field

**Reimert T. (Rei) Ravenholt** — Director, USAID Office of Population, **1966–1979**. This is arguably the single most important operational figure in the entire story. An epidemiologist by training, Ravenholt treated pregnancy as a disease to be eradicated.

**Key Ravenholt actions documented:**
- Advocated sterilizing **one-quarter of all fertile women in the world** to maintain "the normal operation of U.S. commercial interests around the world."
- Ordered **64 advanced laparoscope machines** rushed to India during the 1976 Emergency — sufficient to sterilize **12,800 people per day**.
- Created an interlocking directorate of USAID-funded NGOs (IPPF, Population Council, AVS, Pathfinder) with grants often **10 times their previous budgets**.
- Advocated for **prostaglandins as abortifacients** (1968). Prostaglandins function as emmenagogues — substances that induce menstrual flow, often used as covert abortifacients.
- Admired China's coercive one-child policy as an expression of "the collective will of the people" brought about by "peer pressure."
- If democratic governments refused family planning, he **funded NGOs to operate anyway**, or had staff **smuggle contraband contraceptives** into countries where they were illegal.
- Pioneered the **"inundation approach"** — disseminating contraceptives through any outlet, bypassing trained personnel and educational programs. In Bangladesh, pills were sold alongside "cigarettes, bananas and betel nuts."
- **Total USAID contraceptive shipments under Ravenholt (1968–1995):**
  - **10.5 billion condoms**
  - **2 billion cycles** of birth control pills
  - **73 million IUDs**
  - **116 million vaginal foaming tablets**
  - **250,000 units of Norplant** (by 1995)
  - **4 million doses of Depo-Provera** (by 1995)

**Ravenholt's operational style (from later interviews):**
- He "rode roughshod over contractors and grantees." He was a "bully" and "domineering." USAID relationships were "patron-client," not partnership.
- He had **special powers unique in USAID**: authority to approve assignments of population officers in the field AND veto power over bilateral population projects. No funds could be obligated without Ravenholt's approval. No one could be assigned as a population officer without his approval.
- This was "the only example in 30 years of a true unified global program in which the regional bureaus were secondary to a global strategy run out of the central office."
- The **Carter Administration (1979) removed these special powers**. Population became the province of Regional Bureaus like all other sectors.

**Ravenholt's later political activism:**
On **March 4, 1991**, Ravenholt addressed the Washington State Chapter of Zero Population Growth (ZPG) on the topic: **"Pronatalist Zealotry and Population Pressure Conflicts: How Catholics Seized Control of U.S. Family Planning Programs."** During 1976, he noted, "Catholic activists worked diligently to undermine population growth control efforts."

**John Caldwell** — Australian demographer who, with his wife Pat Caldwell, authored *"Limiting Population Growth and the Ford Foundation Contribution"* (London: Frances Pinter, 1986).

**Phyllis T. Piotrow** — Born 1933; worked in reproductive health field since 1960s. Professor at Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health.

## Part 4: The Thirteen Target Countries

The study explicitly named **thirteen "key countries"** that were to receive "primary emphasis" for U.S. population assistance. Together they accounted for **47% of world population increase** at the time (34.3 million out of 73.3 million annual increase, 1970–75).

The document noted: "It should be recognized that at present AID bilateral assistance to some of these countries may not be acceptable."

### 4.1 India

**Bilateral AID Status (1976):** Active but sensitive.

**The Emergency Sterilizations (1975–1977):** The most catastrophic implementation of NSSM 200 logic.
- **10,929,927 sterilizations** performed during the 21-month State of Emergency (January 1975–March 1977). This represented **36.88% of all Indian sterilizations over a 23-year period**.
- **75% were men** (vasectomies preferred for speed).
- **Coercion methods:** Job loss threats, salary withholding, driving license revocation, detention of students whose parents refused sterilization, free medical care suspended without sterilization certificates.
- **Police violence:** In Muzaffarnagar, police opened fire on sterilization resisters, killing 25. In Sultanapur, 13 were killed.
- **Shah Commission** (post-Emergency inquiry) documented 548 complaints of sterilization of **unmarried persons**, including boys under 18 and men over 75.
- **1,774 deaths** linked to sterilization according to the Shah Commission. One academic thesis estimated ~2,000 died from botched procedures.
- **Specific documented deaths:** Hawa Singh (25-year-old widower, snatched off city bus, died days later from infection after forced vasectomy). Shahu Ghalake (beaten and forced to undergo second vasectomy after telling officers he was already sterilized). In Uttawar village (Haryana), November 6, 1976, police rounded up ~800 men from a Meo Muslim-majority village for forced vasectomy; villagers claim multiple deaths from complications/trauma.
- **World Bank loan:** $66 million to India specifically for sterilization, 1972–1980.
- **LBJ precedent:** In 1965, President Johnson **withheld food aid from famine-threatened India** until it agreed to incentivize sterilization.
- **USAID support:** Ravenholt ordered 64 advanced laparoscope machines rushed to India during the Emergency.
- **Robert McNamara** (World Bank President) traveled to India in November 1976 to congratulate Indira Gandhi's government: "At long last, India is moving effectively to address its population problem."

**Sex Selection:** Sheldon Segal of the Population Council instructed Indian doctors at AIIMS in amniocentesis for sex determination in 1975. By the late 1970s, it was being used systematically to abort female fetuses.

### 4.2 Bangladesh

**Bilateral AID Status (1976):** Large-scale assistance possible.

- Annual population growth: 3% in 1960s/early 1970s, declining to 2.5% by 1981–91.
- 1974 famine revealed pressures of high fertility; government placed high priority on population in First Five-Year Plan (1973–1977).
- January 1976: Government declared rapid population growth the **number one problem** of the country.
- June 1976: National Population Policy outline approved; male and female field workers to provide family planning services door-to-door in rural areas.
- Contraceptive prevalence: 8% in 1975, rising to 25% by 1985. Fertility declined from 6.3 to 5.5 births per woman.
- USAID/foreign aid financing of contraceptives helped avert **14.4 million births between 1974–1990**.
- **Coercion documented:** During famine, field workers fearing loss of salary or dismissal would **deny food to destitute women who did not help them meet sterilization targets**. The Bangladesh army would round up hundreds of people for forcible sterilization. Emergency aid from the World Food Program meant to feed people made destitute from floods was **denied to those who would not agree to sterilization**.
- **Sterilization deaths (1979–1980):** 20 sterilization-attributable deaths identified in Dacca and Rajshahi Divisions, January 1979–March 1980. Death-to-case rate: 21.3 deaths/100,000 procedures. Leading causes: anesthesia overdosage (tubectomy), scrotal infection (vasectomy). Tetanus accounted for 24% of tubal ligation deaths. Three men from the same village died from scrotal infections after vasectomy by the same surgeon at a single clinic (July 19, 1979).

### 4.3 Pakistan

**Bilateral AID Status (1976):** Active.

- One of the first large-scale national family planning programs, predating UNFPA and World Bank involvement.
- USAID was "deeply involved, and in reality was probably a step or two ahead of its own legal authorization."
- Early 1960s assistance rendered under rubric of **health** (Office of Population not created until 1967).
- USAID paid for nearly all nonlocal costs (and some local costs) of first and second programs; maintained large resident advisory staff.
- "Pakistan served as a training ground for USAID's later efforts in other countries."
- USAID adopted Ravenholt's "inundation scheme" — the purest example of USAID's supply-oriented philosophy.
- March 1975: USAID-supported "contraceptive inundation" program launched; oral contraceptives and condoms made abundantly available at affordable prices.
- 1970–75 crude birth rate ~45.5/1000, crude death rate 15.5/1000, growth rate 3.0%.
- By 1977, contraceptive prevalence in most successful project area (comprehensive health + family planning) was 18% (control area: 5%; FP-only area: 2%).
- **Sialkot Study:** A pilot study testing Ravenholt's supply-side hypothesis. Was not allowed to complete before massive inundation program launched. Population officer **Andy Haynal** (physician, former Ford Foundation, Seventh Day Adventist) was architect/manager. Mission Director **Joe Wheeler** had a "gambling streak"; willing to test Ravenholt hypothesis but had open mind.
- Pakistan may have been the **single largest USAID population program in the world** during the mid-1970s.

### 4.4 Nigeria

**Bilateral AID Status (1976):** Little/no interest.

- Second National Development Plan (1970–1974) contained chapter on population policy; gist was that demographic situation did **not** call for emergency action.
- 1975: National Population Council created after several delays; by mid-1970s none of second plan's population policy actions carried out.
- As of 1974, family planning services available only commercially through pharmacies, private maternity homes, university-operated studies, or Family Planning Council of Nigeria (IPPF affiliate).
- **Ishan Experiment** (1969–1972) in Midwestern State: covered ~300,000 population; hospital-based service delivery + full-time community motivators; contraceptive prevalence rose from 1% to 24% in four years.
- **Calabar Rural Maternal and Child Family Health Project** (1975–1980): Cross River State, ~200,000 people in 280 villages; initiated by State Ministry of Health and **Population Council with UNFPA financial support**.
- Calabar was part of international program testing methods of delivering maternal care and FP services to rural populations lacking health infrastructure; four countries participated: **Nigeria, Turkey, the Philippines, and Indonesia**.
- Nigerian version described as best reflecting original intent; results mixed: 63% of women remained uninformed about modern FP as of 1979 survey; ever-use 18% among those who discussed contraception with project personnel vs. 8% among all respondents; discontinuation rate high, only 2% of all women aged 25–54 using a method at time of 1979 survey.
- U.S. bilateral economic assistance to Nigeria: ~$360 million by 1974; total by late 1970s ~$445 million; concessional assistance phased out June 1974 due to oil revenue increase.

### 4.5 Mexico

**Bilateral AID Status (1976):** Private/multilateral only.

- Prior to 1972, government saw population growth as positive; pro-natalist national policy enacted 1936.
- 1974: President Luis Echeverria established CONAPO (National Population Council) to oversee a government-sponsored national family planning program.
- 1974: General Population Law codified Family Planning as solution to burgeoning population.
- 1977: President Jose Lopez Portillo; CONAPO developed first population program with explicit goals of promoting slower population growth through decreased fertility.
- TFR dropped from 6.6 in 1970 to 2.8 by 1995; contraceptive prevalence more than doubled from 30% in 1976 to 66% in 1995.
- Shift toward longer-term methods: 1976: 9% sterilized, 36% oral contraceptives; by 1995: 42% sterilized, 22% IUD, 13% traditional, 13% oral, 5% injectables.
- USAID first provided support to Mexico's population program in **1978**.
- 1985–1995: USAID average yearly contribution **$10 million**.
- 1996: U.S. assistance totaled $13 million (~10% of Mexico's national FP budget of $124 million).
- USAID assistance provided through ~20 U.S.-based NGOs (cooperating agencies) to Mexican governmental institutions, MEXFAM, and FEMAP.

### 4.6 Indonesia

**Bilateral AID Status (1976):** Respond to requests.

**Indonesia's Oil Context — Pertamina and Caltex:**

While not directly part of the population program, the scale of Indonesia's oil industry during the NSSM 200 period is relevant to understanding U.S. strategic interest in the country:

- By early 1975, Pertamina (Indonesia's state oil company) was operating **7 oil refineries** (400,000 barrels/day capacity), **2,680 domestic gasoline stations**, and **29 joint-ventures and subsidiaries**.
- 1972 annual report: 40,000 employees.

- 1974-75 budget: Rp. 777 billion (**$1.9 billion**), equal to about half the government budget; taxable net profits not expected to exceed Rp. 2 billion ($5 million).
- **Caltex Pacific Indonesia:** Largest company in industry, producing 900,000 barrels/day; government proposed collecting extra $1.00 out of Caltex's estimated $2.00–$2.30 profit per barrel = ~$300 million of Caltex's $600–$700 million annual earnings.

This massive oil production — and U.S. corporate involvement in it — helps explain why Indonesia was one of the 13 "key countries" selected for population assistance, despite having a government-led program that the U.S. could support rather than impose.

- 1967: President Suharto signed UN Declaration on World Population with 29 other heads of state, demonstrating "strong political will to reduce population growth."
- 1968: Presidential Instruction No. 26/1968 created National Family Planning Institute (LKBN).
- 1970: BKKBN established under Presidential Regulation No. 8/1970; listed among priorities in New Order's first five-year development plan (Repelita).
- BKKBN infancy: government provided $1.3 million + $3 million from international donors.
- 1977: $28.5 million from state budget; 1978: $34.3 million.

- 1974: Reorganized to make decentralization more effective.
- 1978: Another reorganization.
- 1979: Village Contraceptive Distribution Centers (VCDCs) established.
- **World Bank lending:** First four projects (1972, 1977, 1980, 1985) totaled **$122.4 million**. Nearly all lending to BKKBN. ~40% for headquarters/warehouses/training facilities in Jakarta + 27 provincial + 301 district capitals; 26% for transport/IEC/equipment; remaining third for materials/curricula/research/evaluation/salaries/consultants.

- In 1970s, USAID donated **millions of pill cycles per year** to Indonesia.

- 1984: Joint decree by Ministry of Manpower, BKKBN, APINDO (employers), SPSI (workers) committed to "enhance the implementation of the family planning program amongst workers in the organized sector."

### 4.14 Peru — The Later Catastrophe (1996–2000)

Peru was not one of the original 13 target countries in the 1970s, but it became one of the most devastating cases of NSSM 200-style population policy implementation decades later.

- **~300,000 women** (mostly Indigenous, poor, illiterate) forcibly sterilized under President **Alberto Fujimori** (1990–2000).
- Doctors received **$4–$10 bonuses per sterilization**; promotions tied to quotas.
- **USAID, UNFPA, and the Nippon Foundation** supported the Fujimori government's sterilization efforts.
- In **June 2002**, Peru's congressional subcommittee explicitly identified NSSM 200 as "the global strategy defined for the last quarter of the last century by the United States government in order to obtain a decrease in the birth [growth] rate" and held it responsible for USAID's involvement.
- In **October 2024**, the UN CEDAW committee ruled that Peru's forced sterilization policy could constitute a **crime against humanity** under the Rome Statute.
- **Documented deaths:** Juana Gutierrez Chero (died ~10 hours after sterilization); Celia Ramos Durand (coma, died ~2 weeks after); Magna Morales Canduelas (died 12 days after); Alejandrina Tapia Cruz (died 1 week after); Reynalda Betalleluz (died day after); Josefina Vasquez Rivera (died day after). Victoria Espinoza: Sterilized following C-section; baby also died.
- **Food-for-sterilization exchanges documented.** Ernestina Sandoval (Sullana) was told she could get free food by going to the local hospital; when she got there, she was told she had to be sterilized in order to receive the food.

## Part 6: The Pharmaceutical Pipeline

NSSM 200's Section IV and Section V explicitly called for expanded research into "simple, low-cost, effective, safe, long-lasting and acceptable methods of fertility control" and recommended a **$60 million increase** in federal biomedical research funding.

What actually happened in the field was something else entirely.

### 6.1 The Dalkon Shield Dump

**The Product:** The Dalkon Shield (manufactured by A.H. Robins) was an IUD with a "wicking" string that conducted bacteria into the uterus, causing massive infections, sterility, and deaths.

**The Corporate Deal:**
- A.H. Robins bought manufacturing rights in June 1970 for **$750,000 + royalties**.
- Inventor Hugh Davis' share: $242,812 + $20,000/year consulting fee + future royalties totaling ~$725,000 by 1974.
- Cost to Robins: **$0.35/unit**; sold to physicians for **$4.35**; physicians charged patients ~**$12**.
- Robins captured **60% of US IUD market** in three years: **2.2 million sold in US**, **1.7 million sold abroad**.

**The USAID Export:**
- By 1972, U.S. sales were collapsing due to injury reports.
- In 1973, A.H. Robins' director of international marketing, **Robert W. Nickless**, offered USAID a special deal: **unsterilized** Dalkon Shields in bulk packages at **48% off** — explicitly for "less developed" countries only.
- USAID, under Ravenholt, **approved the deal**.
- The devices were shipped in shoe-box-sized cartons of 1,000 units each. Robins provided:
  - One instruction manual per 1,000 units (in English, French, Spanish only).
  - One applicator per 10 units (vs. one sterile disposable applicator per unit in the U.S.).
  - **No sterilization** (practitioners expected to soak them in disinfectant solution).
- **700,000 units** were obtained by USAID. Over half went to IPPF; the rest to Pathfinder, the Population Council, and international aid programs.
- The devices went to **42 countries**.

**Pathfinder Distribution by Country:**
- 1973: Indonesia (500), Kenya (5,000), Nigeria (1,000), Tunisia (5,200), Dominican Republic (4,000), El Salvador (2,000), Haiti (350), Jamaica (1,000), Venezuela (5,000).
- 1974: Israel (500), Senegal (200), Indonesia (500), Tunisia (7,500), Mexico (1,152), Brazil (1,200), Chile (1,500), Colombia (1,000).
- Substantial quantities also shipped to India, Paraguay, Egypt, Singapore, Thailand.
- Thailand specific: USAID contract **PIN #3012** specifically funded Pathfinder to introduce Dalkon Shield into Thailand.
- Pathfinder total documented: At least **37,602** units.

**Health Effects:**
- **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID):** 5-fold increased risk compared to other IUD types.
- **Septic abortions:** 242 documented cases.
- **Ectopic pregnancies, hysterectomies, infertility.**
- **At least 18 deaths** (likely much higher in developing countries where data was not collected).
- **Tens to hundreds of thousands of adverse events** including pelvic infections.
- Field pregnancy rate at one Latin American clinic: **14.8 percent**.
- As of June 1979, researchers confirmed device **still being inserted in Pakistan, India, and possibly South Africa**.

**Litigation:**
- 9,500 cases litigated/settled by 1985.
- Robins eventually settled around **7,600 claims for ~$245 million**.
- Company filed bankruptcy; established $615 million fund.
- American Home Products acquired Robins in 1989 with **$2.48 billion trust** for claims.
- Trust handled 400,000 cases from 80+ countries; paid 170,000 claimants.

### 6.2 High-Dose Birth Control Pills

**The Context:** In 1970, the FDA advised U.S. physicians to prescribe only low-dose pills (50 micrograms estrogen). Sales of high-dose (80 microgram) pills sagged.

**The USAID Switch:**
- In **1973**, USAID switched its procurement from G.D. Searle, Wyeth, and Parke-Davis to the **Syntex Corporation**, buying millions of dollars worth of their **discredited 80-microgram high-estrogen pills** — for overseas use only.
- AID explained the switch as a response to "market conditions." Simply put, the high-dose pills were cheaper.

**Syntex Corporation Background:**
- Submitted compound for birth control pill in November 1951; first orally active progestin (norethisterone).
- By 1970, Ortho, Syntex, Parke-Davis, Eli Lilly, Upjohn, Wyeth, and Mead Johnson all marketing birth control pills.
- Syntex's Norinyl announced with 8-page spread in Obstetrics and Gynecology.
- 1970 Senate hearings on adverse effects of birth control pills: Syntex sales dropped 10%, earnings fell almost 50%; stock from over $80 to under $20.
- **1972: Sales $130 million** (~30% increase from 1971); **net income $18+ million**; oral contraceptives accounted for only 28% of sales and 35% of profits (down from 47% of profits in 1966).
- 1973: Independent marketing team established with 400 salespeople in U.S. and foreign subsidiaries.
- 1976: Syntex's fraudulent toxicology analysis of naproxen led to FDA uncovering extensive scientific misconduct by Industrial Bio-Test Laboratories.

**Health Effects of High-Dose Pills:**
- **Cardiovascular diseases:** Venous thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, stroke.
- **Blood clots:** G.D. Searle received reports of 132 cases of blood clots in Pill users by 1962; 11 resulted in death.
- **Stroke risk:** 1970s studies linked increased ischemic stroke to high-dose estrogen pills; risk additive with smoking and hypertension.
- **Hypertension:** 3- to 6-fold increase in overt hypertension risk.
- **Important note:** These risks pertain to the relatively high-dose pills of the 1960s and 1970s that USAID shipped to developing countries after they were discredited in the U.S.

### 6.3 Depo-Provera

**The Product:** Depo-Provera (manufactured by **Upjohn Co.**) is an injectable contraceptive.

**The FDA Rejections:**
- 1974: Rejected — potential cancer risks.
- 1978: Rejected — malignant breast tumors in beagle dogs.
- 1983: Rejected — after an unusual "Public Board of Inquiry."
- FDA Center for Drugs and Biologics: "Never has a drug whose target population is entirely healthy people been shown to be so pervasively carcinogenic in animals as has Depo Provera."

**The Export Despite Rejection:**
- By 1977, Depo-Provera available in **90 countries**.
- Manufactured abroad (Belgian subsidiary) despite U.S. ban on export as contraceptive.
- Ravenholt called the FDA decision "the tyranny of the beagle dog."
- **1980:** AID panel of external advisors recommended agency make Depo-Provera available to nations requesting it **despite FDA nonapproval**.
- AID helped finance UNFPA and IPPF, both of which purchased Depo-Provera. IPPF itemized expenditures by donor so AID was assured its funds were not used for Depo-Provera purchases.
- **1979:** AID, through IPPF, supplied Depo-Provera to **378,000 women** in Mexico, Sri Lanka, and Bangladesh in experimental research projects.
- AID directed UNFPA to purchase 600,000 doses for Bangladesh and 1 million for Thailand.
- Specific OR (Operations Research) project populations: San Pablo Autopan, Mexico (8,000 people); Sri Lanka (120,000 people); Matlab Thana, Bangladesh (250,000 people).

**Corporate Financials:**
- 1971–1976: Upjohn admitted spending **$4 million** to secure contracts for sale of its drugs in 29 Third World countries; Depo-Provera sales increased dramatically.

**Health Effects:**
- **Bone mineral density loss:** FDA black box warning 2004; osteoporosis risk; greater loss with longer use.
- **Meningiomas (brain tumors):** March 2024 study found using Depo-Provera for over 1 year increased risk by over 5 times.
- **Clinical trials in 1960s-1970s targeted vulnerable populations** including low-income women and women in developing countries, often without proper informed consent.

**Coercion in Distribution:**
- Impoverished women offered chicken and fish oil to persuade them to use Depo-Provera.
- At Khao I Dang refugee camp in Thailand, women who agreed to Depo-Provera were promised a chicken.
- At Kamput refugee camp, injections were "simply compulsory" per ICRC report.
- In Thailand IPPF-sponsored clinic, 60,000 women received injections; each given **60-90 seconds** to make "free choice."
- **Sri Lanka:** Depo-Provera program started 1976 with ~10,000 vials given by SIDA; 1978 evaluation undertaken; 1980 SIDA withdrew assistance.
- **Bangladesh:** Depo-Provera accounted for almost half the contraceptive use in a rural area supplied with wide choice of methods and intensive FP/health services; contraceptive use increased from 10% to 34%.
- **Tonga:** DMPA use 46% in 1986; highest use of any contraceptive; promoted as first-choice method.

### 6.4 Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA)

**The Device:** MVA prototype developed by American who had been convicted of illegal abortion; caught USAID interest in early 1970s.

**The Contract:**
- USAID contracted **Battelle Corporation** in early 1970s to re-engineer MVA prototype for mass production.
- USAID ordered **1,000 MVA kits**, known euphemistically as **"menstrual regulation kits"**, and distributed them to health practitioners worldwide.
- **1973 Helms Amendment** interrupted this; USAID transferred manufacture/distribution to **Ipas**, an American abortion advocacy NGO.
- In spring 1973, Ravenholt and his office finalized manual abortion devices and ordered manufacturing of **10,000 devices**, planning to call method "menstrual regulation."

## Part 7: Coercion — What "Voluntary" Actually Meant

### 7.1 Paragraph 37: The "Alternative View"

The NSSM 200 Executive Summary included a paragraph that presented — without rejection — a far more radical position:

> "There is an alternate view which holds that a growing number of experts believe that the population situation is already more serious and less amenable to solution through voluntary measures than is generally accepted. It holds that, to prevent even more widespread food shortage and other demographic catastrophes than are generally anticipated, **even stronger measures are required** and some fundamental, very difficult moral issues need to be addressed. These include, for example, our own consumption patterns, **mandatory programs**, **tight control of our food resources**."

The document explicitly directs readers to "See the end of Section I for this viewpoint." The full elaboration at the end of Section I has not been located in any searchable source as of this investigation. This is a critical gap.

### 7.2 The 1976 NSC Food Aid Leverage Memo

On **September 23, 1976**, USC Chairman Charles W. Robinson circulated NSC memorandum **NSC–U/N–178** to Committee Members:

> "Some countries receiving our food assistance have population problems but are doing little or nothing about them. If governments do not commit themselves to support effective population programs, we know that very little can be done on this score. Does not our food assistance provide an incentive for action?"

Robinson noted that the **International Development and Food Assistance Act of 1975** had amended P.L. 480 to require the President to determine aid allocations based on whether receiving countries had "voluntary programs to control excessive population growth."

The memo also acknowledged the risks: LDC accusations that the U.S. consumes more than its fair share, nationalistic backlash, and the difficulty of measuring "performance."

### 7.3 The 1976 "Sine Qua Non" Memo

A separate 1976 National Security Council memorandum stated:

> "In some cases, strong direction has involved **incentives such as payment to acceptors for sterilization**, or **disincentives such as giving low priorities in the allocation of housing or schooling to those with larger families**. Such direction is the *sine qua non* of an effective program."

The exact FRUS document number for this specific memo has not been located. It may be in the 1976 annual report attachments or in a separate NSC staff memorandum not yet published in FRUS.

### 7.4 The First Annual Report (July 29, 1976)

Submitted by Charles W. Robinson, this 25-page report contained several critical admissions:

**On India:**
> "We recommend that U.S. officials refrain from public comment on forced-paced measures such as those currently under active consideration in India. The Indian Government's demand for accelerated action is understandable, but there are moral considerations as well as practical obstacles to involuntary sterilization programs... This is not to be confused with a variety of individual and community incentive schemes the Indian authorities have under consideration to promote voluntary sterilization."

**Translation:** The U.S. knew India was conducting forced sterilizations, advised diplomats to stay quiet about it, and distinguished "involuntary" programs from "incentive schemes" — while continuing to fund and equip the Indian program.

**On methods:**
> "Encourage provision of a variety of family planning methods, particularly pills, condoms, and sterilization."

**On incentives/disincentives:**
The report explicitly listed "incentives/disincentives to encourage smaller families" as a program category.

**On funding:**
Recommended AID population funding levels of over $200 million annually (including UNFPA).

**Countries with Significant Birth Rate Reductions (by 1976):**
15 LDCs had already significantly reduced birth rates according to Embassy reports: China, Thailand, Republic of Korea, Colombia, Taiwan, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Tunisia, Singapore, Jamaica, Costa Rica, Panama, Trinidad and Tobago, Mauritius, Barbados. Additional hopeful outlooks: Indonesia, Philippines, and at least parts of India (e.g., Kerala).

**The "Five Major Population Countries" with Little/No Action:**
Brazil, Nigeria, Egypt, Turkey, Ethiopia.

**The Other Eight of the "Big 13":**
India, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Philippines, Thailand, Mexico, Colombia — active programs but performance can be improved in most cases.

### 7.5 Incentives/Disincentives in Practice

By 1977, multiple countries were paying family planning personnel according to numbers of procedures:
- Bangladesh, Egypt, India, South Korea, Pakistan, Taiwan, and Tunisia were all **paying family planning personnel according to the numbers of IUDs inserted**.
- USAID offering to pay hospitals in **Indonesia, Pakistan, and the Philippines** a fee for each sterilization performed.
- In many countries, freelance "motivators" were paid to recruit patients.

### 7.6 The Helms Amendment (1973)

Passed December 1973 as part of the Foreign Assistance Act of 1973 (P.L. 93-189); sponsored by Senator Jesse Helms (R-NC).

Text: "No foreign assistance funds may be used to pay for the performance of abortion as a method of family planning or to motivate or coerce any person to practice abortions."

**The Nixon administration/USAID strongly opposed it**, protesting it was "at odds with the fundamental philosophy of U.S. population assistance policy" and had "seemingly imperialistic and hypocritical overtones." Removing safe abortion from options "could amount to a form of coercion."

USAID argued: "The Foreign Assistance Act explicitly acknowledges that every nation is and should be free to determine its own policies and procedures with respect to population growth and family planning. In contradiction of this principle, the amendment would place U.S. restrictions on both developing country governments and individuals in the matter of free choice among the means of fertility control…that are legal in the U.S."

USAID interpreted it as eliminating assistance for **all abortions** — even to save a woman's life or in cases of rape in war zones. The agency also interpreted the law as a ban on purchasing/distributing MVA kits "for any purpose."

**1994:** The Leahy Amendment clarified that "motivate" shall not be construed to prohibit provision of information or counseling about all pregnancy options including abortion, consistent with local law.

### 7.7 The Percy Amendment (1973)

Senator Charles H. Percy (R-IL) introduced; passed 1973; amended the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961.

Mandated that U.S. development assistance activities "shall be administered so as to give particular attention to those programs and activities which tend to integrate women into the national economies of foreign countries, thus improving their status and assisting the total development effort."

AID's response: **Policy Determination No. 60 in 1974** — specified strategies to include women as part of all Agency plans, sector assessments, preliminary and final project papers.

USAID Office of Women in Development (WID) created in **1974**.

## Part 8: The Domestic Parallel

NSSM 200 was explicitly **international** in scope. Ford rejected the recommendation for a U.S. domestic population goal announcement. However, the same institutional architecture, personnel, and ideological framework operated domestically.

### 8.1 Indian Health Service Sterilizations (1966–1976)

**The Setup:**
Starting in **1966**, Secretary of the Interior **Stuart Udall** used newly available **Medicaid money** to set up sterilization programs at federally funded **Indian Health Service (IHS) hospitals**.

**The GAO Investigation:**
- **November 4, 1976:** U.S. General Accounting Office report HRD-77-3, "Investigation of Allegations Concerning Indian Health Service." Comptroller General **Elmer B. Staats**.
- Senator **James Abourezk** (D-SD), chairman of Senate Interior Subcommittee on Indian Affairs, requested the investigation on **April 30, 1975**.
- There was an **agreement between Senator Abourezk's and Comptroller General Staats's offices that the investigators would not interview sterilized Native women**. The GAO believed "such an effort would not have been productive because recently published research noted a high level of inaccuracy in the recollection of patients 4 to 6 months after giving informed consent."
- Examined 4 of 12 IHS regions: Aberdeen (SD), Albuquerque (NM), Oklahoma City (OK), Phoenix (AZ).
- **3,406 women sterilized** in four regions (1973–1976).
- **36 women under age 21** sterilized despite court-ordered moratorium.
- **142 male sterilization procedures** also reported.

**Sterilization Numbers by Region:**
| Region | Sterilizations | Avg. Childbearing Women/Year | % Sterilized |
|--------|---------------|------------------------------|--------------|
| Aberdeen, SD | 638 | ~13,000 | ~4.91% |
| Oklahoma City, OK | 1,482 | ~21,500 | ~6.89% |
| Phoenix, AZ | 684 | ~10,700 | ~6.4% |
| Albuquerque, NM | 0 (contracted out) | — | — |

**Critical GAO Failures:**
- The GAO did **not interview any Native American women** who had been sterilized.
- Albuquerque contracted out sterilizations to local non-IHS physicians; therefore their region inaccurately added zero procedures to the government count.
- Independent research estimated **25–50% of Native American women** were sterilized between 1970–1976.
- "Contract" physicians were **not required to comply with any federal regulations** (including informed consent).
- Not a single one of the **113 consent forms** reviewed fully complied with HEW regulations.
- Three different consent forms in use:
  - **91** used HSM-83 form (typically for "medically required, rather than voluntary" sterilizations).
  - **10** used standard form 522 (used for all types of surgery).
  - **12** used HAS-83 form (complied with HEW regulations but medical personnel did not adequately document requisite oral presentation).
- Consent obtained through coercion in many instances.
- Forms failed to: indicate basic elements of informed consent presented orally, contain written summaries of oral presentation, contain statement notifying subjects of right to withdraw consent, advise patients that decision would have no effect on federal benefits.

**Dr. Connie Pinkerton-Uri** (Choctaw/Cherokee) independent study found **1 in 4 American Indian women** had been sterilized without consent; IHS "singled out full-blooded Indian women for sterilization procedures."

Pinkerton-Uri reported: "All the pureblood women of the Kaw tribe of Oklahoma have now been sterilized. At the end of the generation the tribe will cease to exist."

**Lehman Brightman** (Lakota scholar) estimated 40% of Native American women and 10% of men sterilized during 1970s; total women sterilized: **60,000–70,000**.

**IHS Working Conditions:**
- Average new IHS recruit: $17,000–$20,000/year, working ~60 hours/week.
- 1974 ratio: 1 doctor to 1,700 reservation Indians.
- Between 1971 and 1974, applications for vacant IHS positions dropped from 700 to 100.
- Sterilization viewed as providing less future work for underfunded/overpopulated IHS facilities.

### 8.2 Title X (Domestic Family Planning)

**1970:** Public Law 91-572, Family Planning Services and Population Research Act; bipartisan approval; Senate unanimous, House 298–32.

**Sponsors:** Rep. James Scheuer (D-NY), Rep. George H.W. Bush Sr. (R-TX); Sen. Joseph Tydings (D-MD), Sen. Charles Percy (R-IL).

Created **Office of Population Affairs (OPA)** under Secretary of HEW.

**1971 appropriation:** $6 million; 1972: ~$62 million.

**1972:** Congress passed bill requiring state Medicaid programs to cover family planning for low-income families; federal government covers 90% of state expenditures.

**1975 Amendment (Pub. L. 94-63):**
- Established appropriations and reporting requirements.
- **Use of funds to conduct or support research prohibited.**
- Required broad range of methods including natural family planning.
- Ensured right of local and regional entities to apply for grants/contracts.
- Economic status not a deterrent for individuals to participate.

**1978 Amendment (Pub. L. 95-613):**
- Established appropriations.
- Provided infertility services and adolescent services.
- Required culturally appropriate information reviewed by community advisory committee.

**Planned Parenthood:** Received the largest of USAID's program grants, averaging between **$12 and $15 million per year by late 1970s**.

**The Birth Control Pill Development:**
- 1948: Planned Parenthood awarded small grant to biologists Gregory Pincus, John Rock, and M.C. Chang.
- 1956: First large-scale human trial in Puerto Rico; **1,500 Puerto Rican women participated without informed consent**; told only that drug prevented pregnancy, not that it was experimental or that they might experience dangerous side effects; hormone levels **20 times higher** than birth control pills on the market today.

### 9.8 The Mexico City Policy and Beyond

**1984:** Announced at UN International Conference on Population, Mexico City, August 6–14, 1984. U.S. delegation headed by **James L. Buckley**, former U.S. senator and president of Radio Free Europe; stated population was a "neutral issue."

Reagan executive order: any NGO receiving U.S. population assistance funds must attest they do not perform or actively promote abortion as means of family planning — **even with non-U.S. funds**.

**1985:** U.S. Congress held back $10 million of $46 million grant to UNFPA (alleged support for coercive abortions in China); withheld $11 million from IPPF; 1986: ceased funding both entirely.

**Implementation details:**
- January 1, 1985: USAID began applying Mexico City policy.
- Restrictive clauses applied only to **foreign NGOs** receiving USAID population funds.
- **IPPF/London** lost $11.5 million grant + $5 million in commodities contracts = **$16.5 million** (~25% of total budget).
- Three court challenges: DXT Memorial Fund Ltd.; The Pathfinder Fund + Population Council + AVS; PPFA (FPIA).
- Appeals court directed USAID and PPFA to negotiate new grant agreement **excluding Mexico City clauses** until case settled; FPIA received $4.7–$9.4 million annually through FY1990 without Mexico City restrictions.
- When court rejected PPFA's constitutional claims in **September 1990**, USAID terminated FPIA's eligibility.

**The pendulum:**
- Clinton (1993): Rescinded.
- Bush (2001): Reinstated.
- Obama (2009): Rescinded.
- Trump (2017): Reinstated and expanded.
- Biden (2021): Rescinded.

## Part 10: Health Effects and Iatrogenic Disease

### 10.1 Documented Deaths and Injuries

**India Emergency (1975–1977):**
- Shah Commission: 1,774 deaths linked to sterilization.
- One academic thesis: ~2,000 died from botched procedures.
- Infections common; specific documented deaths include Hawa Singh (died days later from infection after forced vasectomy). Kartar (cobbler) had infection and pain, couldn't work for months after forced vasectomy.

**Bangladesh (1979–1980):**
- 20 sterilization-attributable deaths identified.
- Death-to-case rate: 21.3 deaths/100,000 procedures.
- Leading causes: anesthesia overdosage (tubectomy), scrotal infection (vasectomy).
- Tetanus: 24% of tubal ligation deaths.

**Peru (1996–2000):**
- Documented deaths: Juana Gutierrez Chero (died ~10 hours after sterilization); Celia Ramos Durand (coma, died ~2 weeks after); Magna Morales Canduelas (died 12 days after); Alejandrina Tapia Cruz (died 1 week after); Reynalda Betalleluz (died day after); Josefina Vasquez Rivera (died day after). (See Part 4.14 for Victoria Espinoza.)
- Food-for-sterilization exchanges documented. Ernestina Sandoval (Sullana) was told she could get free food by going to the local hospital; when she got there, she was told she had to be sterilized in order to receive the food.

### 10.2 Long-Term Health Effects

**Dalkon Shield:**
- 5-fold increased risk of PID.
- 242 documented septic abortions.
- Ectopic pregnancies, hysterectomies, infertility.
- At least 18 deaths (likely much higher in developing countries where data was not collected).
- Tens to hundreds of thousands of adverse events.

**High-Dose Birth Control Pills:**
- Cardiovascular diseases (venous thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, stroke).
- 3- to 6-fold increase in overt hypertension risk.
- These risks pertain specifically to the high-dose pills of the 1960s and 1970s that USAID shipped to developing countries after they were discredited in the U.S.

**Depo-Provera:**
- Bone mineral density loss (FDA black box warning 2004).
- Meningiomas (brain tumors) — March 2024 study found over 5x increased risk with use over 1 year.
- Never approved by FDA for contraceptive use due to cancer concerns, yet shipped to 90+ countries and used in USAID "operations research" projects on hundreds of thousands of women.

### 10.3 AIDS / HIV

**The scientific consensus:** HIV-1 originated from chimpanzees in central Africa (Cameroon, Gabon, Congo, Zaire); crossed to humans via SIVcpz. The crossover occurred in the **1940s or 1950s** — decades before NSSM 200.

First documented HIV case: 1959 blood sample from Bantu man in Leopoldville (Kinshasa), Zaire.

**There is no documented causal link between NSSM 200, USAID population programs, and the origin of HIV/AIDS.** The virus predates the policy by 20+ years.

The only tangential connection: Large-scale injection campaigns in early 20th century west-central Africa may have facilitated early HIV dissemination via unsterilized syringes. French doctors used 6 syringes to inject 80,000 African workers with sleeping sickness medicine.

### 10.4 Smallpox Eradication in India

The intensified smallpox eradication program (1973–1975) ran simultaneously with Sanjay Gandhi's mass sterilization campaign.

- **May 17, 1975:** Last indigenous smallpox case in India (Katihar district, Bihar).
- **June 30, 1975:** Official announcement of smallpox zero status by Health Minister Karan Singh.
- **April 23, 1977:** India certified smallpox-free.

Both programs used door-to-door health worker deployment, government mobilization, and in some cases police/military support. They **competed for resources**.

- **100 million households searched** during 6-day periods each month.
- 575,721 villages and 2,641 cities covered.
- ~33,000 district health personnel and 100,000+ additional field workers.
- ~8 tons of paper forms consumed per nationwide search.
- Rewards for compliance: 100 rupees ($12) for reporting smallpox cases.
- The central government cleared the Bihar Military Police to assist special epidemiological teams; military personnel helped cordon off affected villages.

However, there is **no evidence** that smallpox vaccination was contaminated or linked to sterilization. The same infrastructure was simply deployed for two different purposes simultaneously.

## Part 11: The Modern Continuation

The underlying institutional structure — USAID Office of Population, State Department population coordination, UNFPA and IPPF funding — has remained intact since 1966. The packaging has changed, but the architecture persists.

### 11.1 Family Planning 2020 (FP2020) — 2012–2020

**Origin:** 2012 London Summit on Family Planning, co-hosted by UK government and Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation.

**Goal:** 120 million additional women and girls using modern contraceptives by 2020.

**Core partners:** Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, FCDO (UK), UNFPA, USAID.

**Gates Foundation investment:** $55 million in data programs (PMA2020) + additional $120 million over 3 years announced 2015.

**Results:** 60 million additional users by 2020 (not 120 million); 320 million total users in 69 focus countries.

**Impact claims:** 121 million unintended pregnancies prevented, 21 million unsafe abortions prevented, 125,000 maternal deaths prevented in last year alone.

**13 countries doubled modern contraceptive users since 2012:** Benin, Burkina Faso, Chad, Côte d'Ivoire, DRC, Guinea, Mali, Mauritania, Mozambique, Niger, Senegal, Sierra Leone, Somalia.

### 11.2 Family Planning 2030 (FP2030) — 2021–Present

**Launch:** November 18, 2021.

**Total commitments at launch:** $3.1 billion over 5 years.

**Gates Foundation:** $1.4 billion over 5 years + $8.5 million over 2 years for FP2030 Support Network.

**UNFPA:** $1.7 billion over 4 years.

**National government commitments:** 46 commitment-makers as of November 2021 including India, Nigeria, Pakistan, Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda.

**Goal:** Universal access to sexual and reproductive health services by 2030 (SDG 3 and 5).

**New structure:** Regional hubs in Africa, Asia, Latin America; Governing Board with governments, multilaterals, donors, NGOs, civil society, youth.

### 11.3 USAID Current Programming

**Pathways to Progress for Sexual and Reproductive Health, 2024–2030** — USAID's current framework.

**Three pathways:**
1. Individuals have accurate information, skills, and ability to take action.
2. Health systems provide quality, accessible, people-centered SRH care.
3. Local communities, organizations, institutions, and governments create and foster social norms and policies that support individuals to make and act on their own SRH decisions, free from violence, coercion, and discrimination.

**Funding levels:** FY2014–FY2016 ranged $608–$715 million annually (bilateral + UNFPA contribution).

**Total since 1965:** ~$14 billion obligated to international FP/RH.

### 11.4 Gates Foundation Current Scale

**2025:** Record $8.74 billion annual budget; plans to spend down ~$200 billion by 2045.

**2025:** Largest single initiative: $2.5 billion investment through 2030 for women's health including contraceptive innovation, maternal care, menstrual and sexual health.

**2026:** Historic $9 billion annual payout announced.

**WHO funding:** 2000–2024, Gates Foundation awarded 640 grants to WHO totaling $5.46 billion; 2023 alone: 62 grants worth $450.3 million.

**Gates Foundation is the second largest funder of WHO** after the U.S. government.

### 11.5 COVAX / Global VAX — Structural Parallel

**Global VAX:** December 2021, USG launched $1.8 billion Initiative for Global Vaccine Access; USAID designated lead federal agency.

**Whole-of-government approach:** USAID, CDC, State (OGAC), DOD, HHS, Peace Corps, Treasury, DFC.

**11 surge countries:** Angola, Côte d'Ivoire, Eswatini, Ghana, Lesotho, Nigeria, Senegal, South Africa, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia.

**Leveraged existing platforms:** PEPFAR, USAID Missions, CDC country offices, influenza networks, Peace Corps, DOD.

**Mass campaign tactics:** Music events (Tanzania — 40,000 vaccinations in 5 weeks), school-based vaccination, door-to-door outreach, pop-up sites in markets/churches/village meeting places.

**U.S. donated 687 million vaccines** to 117 countries; $4 billion to COVAX.

**Gates Foundation role in COVAX:** Influenced vaccine procurement, lobbied against IP waivers, convinced Oxford to partner with AstraZeneca rather than open license.

### 11.6 India's Modern Coercive Population Policies

Two-child norms active in multiple states: Assam (2017), Gujarat (2005), Maharashtra (2003), Odisha (1993), Rajasthan (1992), Uttarakhand (2002), Telangana/Andhra Pradesh (1994).

Penalties include limits on eligibility for contesting elections, entering government service, receiving certain benefits.

Uttar Pradesh Population (Control, Stabilisation and Welfare) Bill, 2021 — most recent federal-level attempt.

Over 35 two-child policy bills tabled in Parliament since Independence.
